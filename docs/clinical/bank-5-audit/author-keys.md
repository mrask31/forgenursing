## qb5-h01 — Basic Care and Comfort

Distinction: `pressure-redistribution-does-not-replace-individualized-repositioning`

Teaching rule: Repositioning remains necessary on a pressure-redistribution surface; individualize the regimen to tissue response and tolerance.

Boundaries: Not a universal two-hour rule; no unstable spine, shock, movement restriction, refusal, or end-of-life comfort trade-off in these cases. Distinct from the existing no-massage family.

## qb5-h01 · Original

Category: Basic Care and Comfort

A stable adult cannot reposition independently and is at risk for pressure injury. A pressure-redistribution mattress has been installed. Which plan best addresses ongoing pressure exposure?

A. Continue assisted repositioning on a schedule individualized to skin findings, tolerance, and the support surface.

B. Reposition only when the client reports pain over a pressure point.

C. Stop scheduled repositioning while the pressure-redistribution mattress is functioning.

D. Use one overnight position if the skin is intact at bedtime.

**Author key: A**

Clinical cue: The client cannot shift weight without help.

Transfer target: Initial plan selection; support surface is the tempting substitute.

- A (correct): The mattress supplements an individualized pressure-relief plan.

- B (incorrect): Pain alone is an inadequate trigger, especially when sensation or communication is limited.

- C (incorrect): A support surface does not remove the need to change pressure loading.

- D (incorrect): An intact bedtime assessment does not justify prolonged unchanged loading.

## qb5-h02 — Basic Care and Comfort

Distinction: `unconscious-oral-care-drainage-and-suction`

Teaching rule: For a client unable to protect the airway, perform oral care with drainage positioning and controlled suction rather than free liquid or an expected rinse-and-spit.

Boundaries: Stable spontaneously breathing adults; no intubation, cervical restriction, or contraindication to side positioning. Not an airway-emergency algorithm. Do not insert fingers to force the mouth open.

## qb5-h02 · Original

C. Turn the head to the side as appropriate and have working suction ready to remove excess fluid.

D. Place the client flat on the back and provide a cup of rinse water.

**Author key: C**

Clinical cue: The client cannot rinse, spit, or protect the airway reliably.

Transfer target: Preparation and equipment, not a memorized amount of rinse.

- A (incorrect): An unconscious client cannot reliably follow a spit instruction.

- B (incorrect): Dependence calls for assisted mouth care with precautions, not indefinite omission.

- C (correct): Drainage positioning and removal of excess fluid address the airway risk.

- D (incorrect): Flat positioning and free rinse water encourage pooling.
